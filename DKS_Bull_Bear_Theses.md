## Consensus baseline

- **What it is.** The largest US sporting goods retailer. The DICK'S Business is guided to $14.6B of FY26 sales, 66% of the total. In September 2025 it bought Foot Locker, guided to $7.45B (34%) [F: Q2 FY26 guide†].
- **Narrative.** A share-gaining category leader whose Foot Locker bet broke in 2026. On Aug 25 non-GAAP EPS guidance fell to $11–12 from $13.50–14.50, and the stock fell 31% that day [F: Q2 release; SGB].
- **Sell-side.**
  - Mean target $166 across 26 analysts, with a Buy consensus [F: stockanalysis†].
  - Post-Q2 targets run $99–205; at least four firms cut to Hold [F: Stocktwits; MarketBeat].
- **Lean.** Ratings still lean bullish, but estimates are falling (Zacks Rank #5†).
  - Short interest is 13.7% of float and declining [F: Benzinga†].
  - Insiders, the CFO among them, bought ~$5M at $123–131 [F: Form 4 summaries†].
- **Price implies.** −0.3% perpetual growth after a ~$1.66B FY27 EBIT anchor, at a 9% WACC. Equivalently, Foot Locker earns nothing and the DICK'S margin is 10.2% [E: evaluation App. A].
- **Live debate.** Are Q2's promotions a one-season clearance, or a lasting reset of DICK'S margins as Nike re-floods wholesale? And can Foot Locker earn anything?

## Dependency note

- **Promotions.** S1, S5, S8 and S9 are largely one bet on promotional intensity and should not be counted four times. S8's EPS gap is mostly S1's margin plus S2's Foot Locker.
- **Nike's wholesale push.** L2, L9 and L3 lean on the same upstream driver.
- **Comps.** On both sides, comps (S6, L3) and ticket (S9, L10) decompose the same revenue line.
- **Net effect.** Each side holds about four independent bets, not ten.

## Where the case stands

The bear side has the stronger evidence-weighted case today: it averages 12.2 against 11.4 for the bull, inside the symmetry band. The gap rests on S1: the refund-masked ~190bp slide in the core margin is the best-documented claim on either side.

The deciding variable is the DICK'S Business margin without refunds. The price already discounts most of the bear case (10.2% implied, with Foot Locker at zero), which is why the evaluation passes rather than shorts.

What would flip the read is an FY27 guide (~Mar 2027) with a DICK'S margin at or above 11% without refunds and a profitable Foot Locker. The bull side's best claims (L2, L3) are dated and cost-side, so that same guide tests both sides at once.
