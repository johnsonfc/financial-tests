## Consensus baseline

- **What it is.** FTAI refurbishes and exchanges CFM56 engine modules for 737NG and A320ceo operators. That business is 87% of TTM revenue and 63% of segment EBITDA. FTAI also runs a leasing book it is moving into managed third-party funds (SCI), and is launching CFM56-based 25 MW gensets for data centers [F: Q2'26 release; E].
- **Narrative.** An aftermarket compounder taking share in CFM56 maintenance, with an asset-light leasing pivot and a power growth leg.
- **Sell-side.** 9 Buy / 2 Hold; mean target $315.67; firm targets after Q2 run $300–375 [F: MarketBeat 9/23/26; firm notes†].
- **Lean.** Ratings lean heavily bullish; positioning doesn't.
  - Short interest is 10.4% of float, up from 6.1% [F: MarketBeat†].
  - The stock is 48% below its 2/26/26 high [F: TradingView].
  - A director sold $61.5M in May [F: Form 4†].
- **Price implies.** $167.03 is 9.6x 2027 plan EBITDA. That means the $2.3B segment plan arrives on time and then grows 1.9% a year forever, at a 10% WACC [E: reverse DCF, evaluation App. A].
- **Live debate.** Is the Aerospace Products (AP) margin slide (35% → 28.5% in two quarters) mix or price? And is Power a durable second engine, or a one-customer boom with a Chinese partner?

## Dependency note

Several bull theses are one bet counted more than once:
- L1, L7 and L9 all depend on airlines accepting FTAI's used- and PMA-based modules at scale.
- L4 is just the valuation of L1 and L2 being delivered, so it is not independent of them.
- L6 depends on the same 2026 FCF that S6 doubts.

The bear side has the same problem:
- S3 and S4 are one bet against Power (execution and policy).
- S2, S5 and S10 share one upstream driver: competitive and OEM pressure on FTAI's pricing.
- S1 and S9 both hinge on when the CFM56 run-off starts.

## Where the case stands

On today's evidence the bear side is modestly stronger, averaging 13.7 versus 12.1 out of 20. That gap is within the symmetry band, and it reflects evidence rather than effort. The bear side's core facts are already reported:
- AP margin is down ~6pp, with 22–24% incremental margins.
- Leasing is running below its 2027 target.
- The FCF guide is back-loaded.
- A new executive order could reach Jereh-packaged turbines.

The bull side's best evidence is also hard data: share rose from 10% to 14%, and GE has cut its retirement assumption three times. But most of its upside depends on delivery that hasn't happened yet.

The deciding variable is the AP EBITDA margin over the next two prints. It separates mix from price, sets 2027 AP EBITDA, and is the best observable proxy for how durable the franchise is. The case would flip to the bulls on AP margin ≥30% in Q3 and Q4'26 with ≥300 modules a quarter, plus DOE rules (due 12/24/26) that exempt behind-the-meter or US-assembled gensets.
