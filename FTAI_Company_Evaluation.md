## 0. Verdict

**Pass.** The variable that matters most is how long FTAI's post-2027 earnings last. The Street's mean target ($316) prices ~16.8x the 2027 plan's EBITDA, which equals ~5.4% perpetual growth after 2027. At $167 the market already assumes the 2027 plan ($2.3B segment EBITDA) arrives on time and then grows ~1.9% a year forever. My base case is a long CFM56 harvest (≈ −2%/yr equivalent), worth ~$90–105 a share [E: reverse DCF, 10% WACC, App. A].

My 24-month probability-weighted value is ~$181 (+8%, ≈4%/yr), below a ~10% cost of equity. The skew is to the right: bull $306 (+83%, p=25%), base $172 (+3%, 45%), bear $89 (−46%, 30%) [E: App. A].

I'm not short: the next 12–18 months of catalysts (module growth, first power deliveries, 2027 guidance) lean positive, and the CFM56 decline shouldn't show before ~2028 [J]. Confidence is ~60%. I'd buy below ~$120, or on ≥$1B of new power orders for 2028+ plus Aerospace Products margins above 30%. I'd revisit a short if 2027 guidance lands below $2.1B with the stock above $200.

## 2. Customer

**Who pays.** Aerospace Products (AP) is paid for by airlines (increasingly "large airline customer deals" [F: Q1'26 call]), MROs, lessors, and FTAI's own Strategic Capital Initiative (SCI) aircraft funds. SCI buys maintenance under "MRE" contracts: $182.8M in Q2'26, 21% of AP revenue [F: Q2'26 release; E].

**Who uses and decides.** Engine shops use the product; airline tech-ops and finance decide, subject to lessor rules on used and non-OEM (PMA) parts [J].

**Concentration** isn't disclosed in accessible data; the last data point is 178 modules to 30 customers in 2023 [F: FY23 release]. The effective top AP customer is an affiliate (SCI). Power has a single hyperscaler customer [F: J&F release, 7/22/26].

**Why they buy.** A module swap takes 5–25 days versus 120–180 for a conventional shop visit [F: FTAI materials via In Practise†]. That saves ~3–6 months of spare-engine rent [E: (120–180) − (5–25) days] at ~1.04% of engine value a month [F: Willis Lease Q1'26]. PMA adds ~$2M of savings per shop visit once all five Chromalloy parts are approved; three were approved by Oct 2025 [F: In Practise†; Avitrader 10/31/25].

**Switching costs** are near zero for spot buyers, but higher under MRE contracts and GMF AeroAsia's five-year capacity guarantee [F: GMF release, 9/23/26].

## 3. Product

**The job.** Restore a CFM56-7B (737NG) or CFM56-5B (A320ceo) engine at the lowest combined cost of parts and downtime. FTAI tears down used engines, refurbishes modules in Montreal, Miami, Rome and Lisbon, and sells or exchanges modules and whole engines [F: Q1'26 call]. In 2026 it launched FTAI Power, which turns CFM56 cores into 25 MW "Mod-1" gensets for data centers (first delivery targeted for end-2026). It sells them through J&F Power Systems, a joint venture with China's Jereh Group [F: Barclays conference†, 5/6/26; J&F release; Q2'26 call].

**Alternatives:** an OEM or licensed shop visit (GE, Safran, MTU, Lufthansa Technik, or StandardAero, whose engine services booked $5.35B in 2025 at a 13.3% company EBITDA margin [F: StandardAero FY25]); an in-house shop; a green-time engine lease (Willis Lease); used parts (AerSale); or parking the aircraft, likelier with jet fuel at ~$152/bbl [F: IATA, June 2026].

**Value chain.** FTAI sits between CFM and large airlines. CFM controls new parts, life-limited-part pricing and repair licenses, and now supplies FTAI under a multi-year materials agreement [F: Barchart†]. Large airlines trade volume for price. In Power, FTAI sits between a Chinese packager and a hyperscaler [J].

## 4. Revenue Unit

**The equations:**
- **AP revenue** = modules × revenue per module, plus MRE revenue. In Q2'26, $875M (including $182.8M of MRE) over 296 modules is ~$2.96M of revenue and ~$844K of EBITDA per module [E: 875/296; 249.7/296].
- **Leasing** = assets × lease rate + reserves + gains + SCI fees and co-investment returns.
- **Power** = units × package price (undisclosed).

**Last three years: volume.** Modules went from 178 (2023) to 757 (2025), with 1,200 targeted for 2026 [F: FY23 release; Q4'25 call; Q2'26 call]. Revenue per module rose ~11% YoY, consistent with heavier workscopes [E: $491.6M/184 → $875M/296].

**Next three years: share.** Share must rise from 14% toward a 25% target [F: Q2'26 call] while GE expects CFM56 shop visits to hold at ~2,300–2,400 a year through 2027, then decline gradually [F: GE Q4'25 call 1/22/26; Aviation Week†]. Power units and SCI fees are the new terms.

## 5. Profit Engine

**The mechanism.** Profit is an arbitrage: buy used engines, harvest serviceable parts, substitute PMA parts, and sell restored modules below the OEM shop-visit price.

**Margins are falling.** AP margin went from 34.7% (2025) to 29.9% (Q1'26) to 28.5% (Q2'26) [E: 671.3/1,936.2; F: Q1'26 call; Q2'26 release]. Incremental margins on YoY revenue gains were only 24% and 22% [E: ΔEBITDA/ΔRevenue].

Management blames mix — more full-performance restorations, which earn ~25% margin on a 10,000-cycle engine versus ~40% on a 6,000-cycle one. It also calls it a deliberate choice of share over margin [F: Q2'26 call; Laguna conference 9/16/26]. That is plausible. It is also what an aging fleet plus pricing for share looks like [J].

**Profit concentration.** Leasing earns 37% of segment EBITDA on 13% of revenue. EBITDA excludes its depreciation, and it includes one-offs: Q1'26's ~$153M had $45M of insurance recoveries and $12M of gains [F: Q1'26 call].

**ROIC vs. WACC.** ROIC is ~19–22% TTM [E: NOPAT $0.71–0.82B ÷ ~$3.7B invested capital — total assets $4.49B† less cash and an assumed ~$0.45B of non-interest-bearing liabilities — at 17.8% tax]. WACC is ~10% [J: 4.75% risk-free, 4.14% ERP (F: Damodaran 9/1/26), beta ~1.3–1.6]. No five-year series was buildable from accessible data, and excess returns are only ~2 years old.

**Tests.** Excess ROIC is ~2 years old, and performance through a downturn is untested. No price increase stuck: margins fell ~6pp while share rose from 10% to 14% [F: Q1'26 and Q2'26 calls].

**No proven moat.** The advantage is real but tied to the cycle: stable through 2027, eroding after. The threats are Willis Lease's "Module Shop" (first CFM56-7B core restoration, Feb 2026 [F: Willis Lease 2/11/26]), StandardAero's CFM56 center, and CFM's control of supply.

## 9. Macro Tailwinds / Headwinds

1. **Fuel shock** (cyclical; partly priced in). Iran-war disruption has jet fuel at ~$152/bbl, and IATA halved 2026 airline profit to $23B, a 2.0% margin [F: IATA, June 2026]. It works through maintenance budgets and aircraft parking: near term it favors FTAI's discount offer; later it pulls CFM56 retirements forward.
2. **Delivery shortfalls vs. LEAP fixes** (structural; consensus extrapolates it [J]). GE cut its CFM56 retirement assumption to 1.5–2% from 3–4% [F: GE 1/22/26; Aviation Week†]. But the LEAP-1B durability kit (~2x time on wing) is in full production from 2027 [F: Leeham 7/16/26]. Tailwind through 2027, headwind after.
3. **Rates** (cyclical headwind; partly priced in). The Fed hiked to 3.75–4.00% on 9/16/26 and projects more; the 10-year is ~4.75–4.8% [F: FOMC; Damodaran]. The channel is WACC, SCI fundraising and securitization spreads, and the 2028 refinancing.
4. **Data-center power** (cyclical tailwind; priced in only if durable). A $1.465B hyperscaler order [F: J&F release] lifts Mod-1 demand and pricing, but only while heavy-duty turbines stay scarce [J].
5. **Trade policy** (binary; unpriced). The Section 232 review imposed no aircraft or parts tariffs for now (7/9/26). Updates are due 1/5/27, and action is possible after 180 days [F: KPMG 7/2026]. Exposure: modules flowing into the US from Montreal, Rome and Lisbon.

## 11. Contrarian Case (the long)

In a long PM's voice, ranked by damage to my pass:
1. "GE keeps revising your 'finite life' against you. 2026 retirements were assumed at 3–4% and are running 1.5–2%, and ~30% of the fleet hasn't had a first shop visit [F: GE; Aviation Week†]. At 4% a year from 2030, ~60% of ~23,000 engines still fly in 2040 [E: 0.98⁴ × 0.96¹⁰; F: StandardAero S-1]. And FTAI's share of them is heading from 14% to 25%."
2. "Power is a franchise, not a fad. A hyperscaler committed $1.465B under a five-year master agreement before the first unit shipped. Management calls $450M 'intentionally conservative.'"
3. "You're paying ~12x 2027 EPS on plan (~$13.50 [E]) and 9.6x EBITDA for 40%+ growth and ~20% ROIC."
4. "July was mechanical: the Russell 2500 deletion [F: index coverage†], a director's sale, an oil shock. Insiders bought, a $500M buyback is authorized, and short interest is 10.4%."

**Rebuttals:**
1. **Partly conceded.** My base grows AP to 2028 and holds it flat to 2032, and value barely moves with the run-off shape ($89–104 [E: App. A]). The gap is perpetual versus finite life. *Weak:* if share gains outrun the decline into the mid-2030s, my plateau is too short, though each extra two years adds only ~$5–8/sh.
2. **Moderate.** Nothing has shipped, FTAI's economics are undisclosed, and the partner is Chinese. Pricing rests on turbine shortages that OEMs are expanding capacity to end. Even $750M a year through 2036, then fading, is worth only ~$4.3B (~$41/sh) [E: App. A]. *Weak:* if Power lasts 15+ years, my case fails.
3. **Strong.** "On plan" assumes the plan. The comparables own IP or span many platforms; FTAI depends on CFM's IP and one engine family.
4. **Valid.** That's why I'm not short, but it's no reason to own the stock at ~4% a year.

**P(the long side is right over 36 months) ≈ 35%.**

**Pre-mortem.** *It is October 2029 and passing cost us 40%+; FTAI is ~$240.* The likeliest reasons:
1. The hyperscaler re-ordered, and Power became a recurring ~$700M line.
2. OEM delays kept CFM56 retirements under 2.5%, and AP hit ~$1.7B at ~30%.
3. The market paid 12x+ while growth lasted, and I mistook July's index flows for signal.
