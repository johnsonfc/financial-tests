## 0. Verdict

**Pass.** The variable that matters most is the DICK'S Business operating margin once one-off tariff refunds are removed.
- Q2's 12.6% included a $59M IEEPA refund. Without it the segment earned ~11.1%, against 13.0% a year earlier [E: ($485.2M − $59M) ÷ $3.85B; F: Q2 FY26 release and 10-Q, 8/25/26†].
- If Foot Locker never earns a dollar, $136 implies a DICK'S margin of 10.2% from FY27. My base case is 10.8%, with Foot Locker reaching a 2.5% margin by FY30. That is worth ~$163, close to the Street's $166 mean target [E: App. A].

My 24-month probability-weighted value is ~$172, or +27% with dividends (≈12.5%/yr):
- bear $95 (−30%, p=30%);
- base $170 (+25%, 45%);
- bull $270 (+98%, 25%) [E: App. A].

That beats a ~10% cost of equity by only ~2.5pp a year, and moving five points of probability from bull to bear erases the excess. Confidence in passing is ~55%.
- **I'd buy** below ~$123, or if the FY27 guide (~March 2027) shows a DICK'S margin of at least 11% without refunds and a profitable Foot Locker.
- **I'd revisit a short** if that guide puts the DICK'S margin below 10% with the stock above $150.

## 2. Customer

- **Who pays and decides.** Families and athletes pay. The athlete or parent decides, but brands decide what reaches the shelf. Foot Locker's buyer is younger, sneaker-led and mall-based [J].
- **Concentration.** No customer concentration. The real concentration is Nike: DKS and Foot Locker together buy ≈$5B, ~10% of Nike's revenue [F: Fortune, 5/19/25†]. Nike's share of DKS purchases was not retrieved.
- **Why they buy.** Breadth, launch access and price-matching for an activity the household already funds.
  - Youth organized-sport participation is 58% of 6–17-year-olds, up from 54% in 2022.
  - Per-child costs are up 46% since 2019 [F: Aspen Project Play, 2025].
  - GameChanger, the team scoring and streaming app, ties families to DKS between purchases [J].
- **Switching cost.** Near zero in dollars; loyalty is assortment-driven [J].

## 3. Product

- **The job.** One trip for equipment, footwear and apparel across sports. House of Sport turns the store into a destination with turf, climbing walls and cages [F: DKS releases].
- **Alternatives.** Brand websites (Nike, Adidas, On, HOKA), Amazon, Walmart and Target. Also Academy (value), JD Sports' Finish Line and Hibbett (sneakers), and doing without.
- **Value chain.** DKS sits beneath stronger suppliers. Nike controls allocation and, after cutting wholesale partners in 2017–23, is shipping to them again: North America wholesale was up ≈9% in Nike's quarter to Aug 31, 2026† [F: Nike Q1 FY27 release, 10/1/26].
- **What that means for DKS.** In Q2, brands' own site promotions "spilled into the broader marketplace" [F: Stack, Q2 call, 8/25/26]. DKS's counterweights are scale (~9% of US sporting goods sales [F: Telsey via Benzinga†]), vertical brands and its retail-media network.

## 4. Revenue Unit

Revenue = stores × sales per store + online. Comps = transactions × ticket.
- **Recent growth.** DICK'S comps were +2.4%†, +5.2% and +4.5% in FY23–25 [F: releases]. FY25 revenue rose 28% only because of the acquisition.
- **Ticket, not traffic.** Q1 FY26's +6.0% was ticket +5.5% and transactions +0.5%† [F: Q1 release]. Ticket (price and mix: premium footwear, House of Sport) drove the last three years.
- **Next three years.** The DICK'S Business needs both terms: ~14 House of Sport openings a year (~75% relocations) plus ticket [F: 10-K†].
- **Foot Locker.** Sales shrink from $7.99B (FY24) to the $7.4–7.5B FY26 guide [F: FL 10-K; Q2 guide†]. Comps must turn positive before closures stop subtracting.

## 5. Profit Engine

- **Where the profit is.** All economic profit comes from the DICK'S Business: $1.64B of FY26 operating income on 66% of sales. Foot Locker and a ~$70M reconciling cost subtract [F: Q2 guide†; E].
- **Gross margin.** The gap is structural: 36.3% at DICK'S versus 24.4% at Foot Locker in FY25† [F: 10-K].
- **Incremental margin is now negative in the core.** Q2 DICK'S sales rose 5.6%, but underlying operating margin fell ~190bp to ~11.1% once the refund is removed [E: Section 0]. Management expects promotions "to continue through the balance of the year" [F: Stack, Q2 call].
- **Reinvestment.**
  - FY26 net capex is ~$1.4B, 2.2x D&A of ~$624M [F: 10-Q†; E].
  - The money goes to House of Sport, Field House and Golf Galaxy Performance Centers, and to ~250 Foot Locker "Fast Break" remodels. The pilot remodels posted double-digit comps [F: Retail Dive, 3/2026†].
- **ROIC vs. WACC.**
  - ROIC peaked at 22.8% in FY21 (pandemic), then ran 16.0%, 14.4% and 14.4% [F: GuruFocus†].
  - FY26 is ~10% [E: NOPAT $1.12B (1,510 × 0.74) ÷ ~$11B capital: FY24's ~$7.8B, implied by 14.4%, plus the $2.5B purchase and ~$0.7B of growth capex].
  - WACC is ~9% [E: App. A]. The deal pulled returns from ~5pp above the cost of capital to about breakeven.

## 6. Moat

- **Scale economies.**
  - *Benefit:* national marketing, vendor terms and retail-media reach.
  - *Barrier:* Academy is under half DKS's size ($6.1B sales [F: ASO FY25 release, 3/17/26]).
  - *Evidence:* the DICK'S Business earns 11.1% against peers at 7–8.5% (Academy 8.5%, JD North America 7.4%†, Hibbett 7.9% pre-acquisition) [F: releases]. It also held positive comps while Academy's turned negative (−1.5% FY25, −0.4% Q2 FY26) [F: ASO releases].
- **Cornered resource: launch allocation.**
  - *Benefit:* premium product.
  - *Barrier:* Nike's discretion, which is not DKS's to keep. Nike's broad wholesale return weakens it [J].
- **Network economies (GameChanger).**
  - *Benefit:* each team's parents join together.
  - *Barrier:* league-level switching. Evidence is thin: management cites "strong growth" without figures [F: Q2 call†].
- **Branding, counter-positioning, process power.** None proven. Foot Locker's banner has negative comps.
- **Tests.**
  - Excess ROIC sustained in FY22–24: passed.
  - Ticket increases that stuck: partly, until Q2's promotions.
  - Share stable in a downturn: passed.
- **Direction.** Stable in the DICK'S Business. Eroding at group level as Foot Locker dilutes returns and Nike widens distribution.
- **Conclusion.** A scale moat worth ~3pp of margin at DICK'S; no proven moat at Foot Locker [J].

## 7. Capital Allocation & Governance

- **Cash returns exceed free cash flow.**
  - H1 FY26: dividends $225M† and buybacks $141M (0.7M shares) [F: 10-Q].
  - FY26 unlevered FCF is ~$0.3B, against a $450M dividend run-rate ($5.00 a share) [E: App. A].
  - The gap is being financed: $1.0B of notes in September 2026, $400M at 6.2% due 2036 and $600M at 6.9% due 2056 [F: FWP/424B2, 9/23/26]. That adds ~$66M of annual interest [E].
- **M&A.** Foot Locker cost $2.5B: $2.1B in stock (9.6M shares), $223M cash and a $112M existing stake [F: Q3 FY25 10-Q].
  - That is 0.31x Foot Locker's FY24 sales and 24x its FY24 operating income of $103M [E].
  - It has since lost $52M in FY25† and is guided to lose $40–80M in FY26 [F: releases].
  - Charges of $500–750M are planned; $390M were booked in FY25 [F: CNBC 11/25/25; FY25 release†].
  - TD Cowen called the deal "a strategic mistake" on announcement [F: CNBC, 5/15/25].
- **Incentive-plan metrics:** not retrieved (proxy blocked).
- **Control.** 23.6M Class B shares, a separate high-vote class, give the Stack family control [F: 10-Q cover; J]. Its exact voting share was not retrieved. Executive Chairman Ed Stack now runs Foot Locker directly [F: Chain Store Age, 9/2025].
- **Insiders bought after the drop.** CFO Navdeep Gupta bought $1.0M (9/22/26); directors bought ~$3.7M in late August†, including William Colombo ($0.9M) and Larry Fitzgerald Jr. ($0.24M) [F: Form 4 summaries].
- **Accounting flags.**
  - Non-GAAP EPS excludes recurring-looking integration costs: FY25 GAAP was $9.97 against $13.20 non-GAAP [F: FY25 release].
  - Q1 FY26 GAAP EPS included a $150M settlement gain [F: 10-Q†].
  - Q2's $59M tariff refund stayed inside non-GAAP results [E].
- **Legal.** A plaintiff firm announced an investigation after August 25†; no suit has been confirmed.

## 9. Macro Tailwinds / Headwinds

1. **Tariffs** (policy; mixed; partly priced). The Supreme Court voided the IEEPA tariffs on 2/20/26 [F: CRS]. The resulting refunds are one-time: $59M plus $2.1M of interest in Q2 [F: 10-Q]. New Section 301 tariffs since 7/24/26 run 12.5% on China and Vietnam† and 10% on Indonesia [F: Holland & Knight]. They hit vertical-brand costs and vendor pricing.
2. **Promotions** (cyclical headwind; priced after Aug 25).
   - Nike's own direct sales fell 8% and its inventory reached $7.8B [F: Nike Q1 FY27].
   - Elsewhere in the channel: JD North America like-for-likes −6.8%† and Academy transactions −5.3% [F: AJ Bell; ASO Q2].
   - Excess brand inventory reaches DKS through price-matching.
3. **Rates** (cyclical headwind). The 10-year was 5.24% on 10/1/26†, up from ~4.8% a month earlier. The Fed hiked to 3.75–4.00% on 9/16/26 [F: FOMC; Appendix B]. The channels are WACC, the new notes' 6.2–6.9% coupons, and household budgets.
4. **Youth sport** (secular; small, positive). Participation is back to 58% [F: Project Play]. This supports DICK'S team categories and GameChanger.
5. **Nike's wholesale return** (structural; mixed). More product flows to Foot Locker, but DKS's relative allocation edge narrows [J].

## 11. Contrarian Case (the long)

Ranked by damage to a pass:
1. **Asymmetry.** $136 already prices Foot Locker as worthless and a ~1pp DICK'S margin cut [E: App. A]. Street fair value is $166 (+22%) [F: stockanalysis†], plus a 3.7% yield [E: $5.00 ÷ $136.08].
2. **Insiders bought ~$5M** at $123–131, the CFO among them [F: Form 4 summaries†].
3. **DICK'S is taking share.** Its comps were +4.9% while Academy was at −0.4% and JD North America at −6.8% [F: Q2 releases; AJ Bell†].
4. **Synergies are cost-side,** within management's control, and not yet in the numbers.
5. **The balance sheet is investment-grade** (Baa2/BBB), with net debt ~0.5x EBITDA excluding leases [F: 10-Q; E].

Rebuttals:
1. **Conceded on value.** The base is worth $163–170 [E: App. A]. The pass comes from the thin excess return, not a downside call. *Weak:* if the bull probability is 30%, I should own it.
2. **Insider buying.** Directors' purchases are small next to the Stack family's control and the $2.1B in stock it issued for Foot Locker [J].
3. **Share gains.** They are being bought with promotions: the underlying Q2 margin fell ~190bp [E: Section 0].
4. **Synergies.** They offset only part of Foot Locker's swing, from +$130M to −$60M within one year [F: guide history].
5. **Balance sheet.** Leverage is rising to fund dividends and buybacks that exceed free cash flow. *Weak:* rated leverage has room.

P(the long is right) ≈ 45%.

**Pre-mortem:** it is October 2028 and passing cost 40%+. The most likely reasons:
- promotions normalized in 2027 and the DICK'S margin rebuilt to 11.5%;
- Fast Break and Nike's launch pipeline returned Foot Locker to 3–4% margins;
- the new notes funded buybacks at trough prices, and the multiple re-rated to 8x.

**Conflicts and how I resolved them:**

- **10-year Treasury.** 5.24% (10/1/26, single source) vs. ~4.8% (9/1/26). I use the later figure for cost of equity, and the 8–11% grid covers both.
- **FY25 net income.** $849M (release) vs. $1,184M (10-K summary, which matches the DICK'S-only margin). I trust the release.
- **FY26 operating-income guide.** One outlet reported it "raised to $1.71–1.83B" at Q2. Two outlets and the release give a cut to $1.45–1.55B, which I use.
- **Consensus EPS.** Zacks' FY26 $13.89 and FY27 $15.31 predate the Aug 25 cut. I treat them as stale and anchor on guidance.
- **Q2 EPS consensus:** $3.76 vs. $3.78. Immaterial.
- **DKS market share:** ~9% (Telsey) vs. 21.3% (undefined base). I use 9%.
- **House of Sport target:** 75–100 stores vs. one unattributed "67 by end-2027." I cite neither as a forecast.
